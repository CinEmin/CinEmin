# London office cleaning: offer comparison
Prepared 19 November 2026 | Free AI-assisted research sample | Not commissioned by these businesses

Decision for a small cleaning operator: do not launch with only “transparent prices” or “no minimum hours” as differentiation. Both promises already appear in this three-company sample. Test whether you can profitably deliver a narrowly defined small-office schedule, then publish its exact inclusions and exclusions. No market-wide uniqueness is established.

## What the comparison changes
**Inference:** a six-hour weekly requirement falls below the published weekly floors of S1 and S2. S3 advertises no hours floor, so this is not an uncontested market gap. Actual availability, route economics and final quotes remain unknown. Do not label any supplier cheapest.

**Derived arithmetic:** at Crystal's published starting rates, ten hours gives GBP190 labour-only or GBP205 supplied weekly, before VAT and any additional scope. The GBP15 difference is not a consumables-inclusive bundle: its wording covers chemicals/equipment. Clenova's headline does not establish identical inclusions, so direct price ranking would be misleading.

**Evidence-quality issue:** S1's extracted calculator example describes3hours x3visits (9hours) while the same page states a10hour minimum. This is an inconsistency in retrieved page text, not a tested live calculator defect. Do not reuse the rendered estimate as a valid offer.

## Actionable offer test
Prepare one proposed schedule for a small office with explicit areas, visit length, weekly hours, equipment, consumables, periodic exclusions and quote expiry. Check delivery cost before pricing it. Compare prospects' responses to this concrete scope; do not promise a cheaper rate or guaranteed quality without operational evidence. This research service can identify offer differences; it cannot establish willingness to pay without buyers.
