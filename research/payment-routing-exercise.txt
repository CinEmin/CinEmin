WHERE DID THE SALE MONEY GO?
A short payment-routing exercise for digital creators
Original AI-assisted educational specimen | 24 November 2026

A sales total is not the same number as an unpaid platform balance. Before
adding numbers, label where the money went. This fictional example uses one
currency (USD). Fees are invented amounts, not a provider fee schedule.
Opening balances are zero. There are no taxes, refunds, holds, foreign-exchange
adjustments or other transactions in this example.

CASE
A: Customers pay USD120. The platform deducts USD24. The remaining money stays
   in the platform account. No transfer to a bank has occurred.
B: Another customer pays USD15. USD3 is deducted. The remaining money goes
   directly to the creator's separate payment account, not the platform balance.
C: A later customer pays USD10. USD2 is deducted. The remaining money stays
   in the platform account. Again, no bank transfer has occurred.

TRY IT BEFORE READING THE ANSWER
1. After A and B, what is the gross sales total?
2. After A and B, how much remains in the platform account?
3. After A and B, how much has reached the separate payment account?
4. After C, what is the platform balance?
5. If the platform has a hypothetical USD100 minimum, can you conclude that
   money will reach the bank tomorrow? Why or why not?

ANSWER
After A and B: gross sales are USD135; deductions are USD27. Of the remaining
USD108, USD96 stays with the platform and USD12 is in the separate account.
Do not add that USD12 to the platform balance: it has already taken another
route. After C, the platform balance is USD104 (96 + 8). Across all three sales,
USD145 gross minus USD29 deductions equals USD116: USD104 at the platform plus
USD12 at the separate account. These are locations of funds, not business profit.
A USD104 balance above an assumed minimum does not establish an eligible payout,
a scheduled transfer or a bank receipt. In real accounts, review, holds, payment settings and
processing can matter. The example proves arithmetic only, not an account status.

USE THE DISTINCTION ON YOUR OWN RECORDS
Keep actual records private. For each relevant transaction, note its date and
time zone, currency, identifier, gross amount, documented deductions, net amount
and settlement destination. Mark an unknown destination UNKNOWN; do not guess.
Use the same reporting period when comparing reports. Track each currency
separately. Record actual refunds and adjustments from statements rather than
assuming the original fee was returned. A sale appearing in two reports is still
one sale. Confirm a bank receipt in the bank record; a platform status is separate.
This is not a tax, bookkeeping-compliance or account-recovery service.
